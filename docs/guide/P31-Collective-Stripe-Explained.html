Your Stripe and the website, explained

What the website does with your Stripe account, and exactly where to see every payment, client and price in your own dashboard.

What “connected with an API” means

An API is a private, secure line between two systems. The website holds a secret key to your Stripe account, the P31 Collective account, so it can ask Stripe to do a few jobs for you. It is like giving your assistant a key to the office: she works inside your space, and everything she does is recorded there. Nothing happens outside your Stripe account, and nothing is hidden from you. Card numbers never touch the website; Stripe keeps them.

The four jobs the website does in your Stripe

1

Opens checkout

When a client chooses her plan, the site opens Stripe’s secure checkout using your product and price, the one behind your Payment Link.

2

Starts the plan

Her monthly subscription (or 6-month payment) is created in your account, under her name and email.

3

Hears “paid”

Stripe sends the site a message the moment she pays. The site then enrolls her, opens her classroom and emails you and the team.

4

Copies your prices

Once a day and at every checkout, the site reads your prices from Stripe, so the private forms page always shows what you set.

The change we just made: before, checkout made a one-off price for each sale, which is why the sales did not appear under your products. Now every enrollment is charged with your own product, so each sale shows under that product in Stripe, and you control the price from Stripe.

Where to find everything in Stripe

Sign in at dashboard.stripe.com and make sure the account name at the top left is P31 Collective. If a menu has moved, type the name into the Stripe search bar at the top; it finds every page below.

Changing a price

  1. In Stripe, open Payment Links and click the plan’s link (or open the product in Product catalog).
  2. Change the price there. Stripe may ask you to add a new price to the product; that is normal.
  3. That’s it. The very next checkout charges the new price, and the website shows it on the private forms page by the next morning.

Good to know

  • Clients already subscribed keep their current price. To change theirs, open Billing → Subscriptions, choose the client, and update the subscription.
  • Keep the same links. If you make a brand-new Payment Link instead of editing one, send the new link to the team so the website points to it.
  • Turning a link off stops it working in Stripe; tell the team so that plan can be hidden on the forms page too.
  • Prices stay private: they show only on each client’s personal enrollment page, never on the public website.

A client’s journey, seen from Stripe

  1. She signs her three forms and chooses her plan on her private page.
  2. She pays on Stripe’s checkout: a new Customer and a payment appear in Transactions, and a subscription in Billing.
  3. Stripe sends the “paid” message (you can see it under Developers → Webhooks).
  4. The website enrolls her and emails her welcome; you and the team get an enrollment email.
